01 AI Strategy

AI Strategy & Business Value

  1. Identify material sources of value

    Find the areas where AI or automation may add the most value.

  2. Select the appropriate intervention

    Match each opportunity with the approach that serves it best — AI, process design, or standard software.

  3. Prioritise the portfolio

    Weigh value, feasibility, data readiness, risk, complexity, and cost.

  4. Establish the investment case

    Include integration, infrastructure, people, oversight, and operations.

  5. Target the right improvements

    Target possible reductions in repetitive work, delays, errors, or cost-to-serve — assessed and measured in context.

  6. Make it operable

    Roadmaps, governance, investment priorities, operating models, and outcome measures.

Cost improvements are assessed and measured in context, so decisions rest on evidence. Where a measured result exists in the project record, it is reported precisely.

06 AI opportunities for growth businesses

AI opportunities for growth businesses

Other engagements — each with a sensible first step and the considerations that set it up for success.

Other engagement

Invoice and document handling

Opportunity: free up staff time from manual extraction and sorting. First step: pilot one document type — extract key fields for staff review. Consider: formats, privacy, human review, integration.

Other engagement

Internal knowledge assistant

Opportunity: help staff find answers in policies and manuals quickly. First step: an assistant over a few approved documents, with links to sources. Consider: content currency, permissions, cited answers.

Other engagement

Customer enquiry support

Opportunity: respond to enquiries faster with prepared drafts. First step: categorise enquiries and draft responses for a person to review and send. Consider: accuracy, customer data, human ownership.

Other engagement

Lead follow-up prioritisation

Opportunity: give every enquiry a timely response. First step: organise enquiries by agreed criteria and flag those needing timely response. Consider: clear criteria, with people deciding the follow-ups.

Other engagement

Stock and demand alerts

Opportunity: keep stock levels well matched to demand. First step: alerts for unusual demand or low-stock risk from existing sales and inventory data. Consider: data quality, seasonality, staff review before reordering.

Other engagement

Scheduling support

Opportunity: build work-order and field-service schedules in minutes. First step: propose schedules from availability, location, and business rules for a person to approve. Consider: real rules, edge cases, acceptance.

Other engagement

Quality inspection support

Opportunity: make visual inspection faster and more consistent. First step: explore computer vision for one defined inspection task with human review and testing first. Consider: image quality, variation, false positives.

Other engagement

Meeting and service-record summaries

Opportunity: spend meeting time on decisions while the summary drafts itself. First step: turn approved recordings or notes into draft summaries and action lists. Consider: privacy and consent, human review, retention policy.

Other engagement

AI readiness and workflow review

Opportunity: gain clarity on where AI delivers the most value. First step: a short workflow review that identifies where process redesign or existing software offers the best path. Consider: cost, capability, change effort.

Each engagement is a well-scoped first step, evaluated on its own merits — building confidence before committing to a larger program.

08 Responsible AI

Responsible AI and production readiness

Responsible AI runs across the lifecycle — governance, privacy, security, human oversight, explainability, auditability, model and prompt evaluation, monitoring, and regulatory readiness. Oversight designed in from the start and monitored after release — AI that is controlled, auditable, and ready to stay in production.

Governance designed for Board assurance, regulatory scrutiny and production accountability.

Frameworks and regulatory domains reflected in professional experience include those listed here — areas of working familiarity gained through hands-on practice.

  • NIST AI RMF
  • ISO 42001
  • EU AI Act
  • GDPR
  • MAS TRM
  • MAS FEAT
  • FDA SaMD
  • HSA
  • HIPAA
  • PDPA

AI Security

Protecting AI systems and the data they touch — secure model and prompt handling, data protection, access controls, adversarial input awareness such as prompt injection, and security review built into evaluation and deployment.

AI Governance

Clear ownership, policies, and checkpoints — intake and prioritisation gates, risk assessment, human oversight and escalation paths, audit trails, and monitoring that keeps models accountable in production.

AI Ethics

Fairness, transparency, and respect for the people affected — bias awareness, explainability, privacy by design, and honest communication about what a system can and cannot do.
